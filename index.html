Delay & EOT on US projects

Time Impact Analysis

Prepared on live delay events, then submitted with the notice or time extension request.

  1. Start from the last accepted update before the event, not the baseline.
  2. Read the records: RFIs, notices, owner directives, daily reports.
  3. Model the event as a fragnet and tie it to the activities it really affected.
  4. Recalculate at the same data date and compare completion and milestones.
  5. Report days of impact, the critical path before and after, and why, in a format reviewers accept.

How I work

From drawings to an approved baseline

The same sequence on every job. It is why baselines pass review and why updates stay meaningful months later.

  1. A1000

    Read the contract

    Milestones, specification (e.g. Division 01 32 16), calendars, weather days, reporting format.

  2. A1010

    Break down the work

    WBS by area, level and system, straight from the drawings and scope, so progress can be measured.

  3. A1020

    Plan procurement

    Submit, review, approve, fabricate, deliver: a chain for every long-lead item, because that is often the real critical path.

  4. A1030

    Tie the logic

    Every activity has a predecessor and a successor. Constraints only where the contract demands them.

  5. A1040

    Load and level

    Resources or cost (BOQ or schedule of values) for S-curves and earned value, when the job needs it.

  6. A1050

    Check, then issue

    14-point health check and a read of the critical path from start to finish before anything goes out.
